# Executive Sales Performance Analysis

### Turning Sales Data into Business Insights

**Project Type:** Data Analytics | Business Intelligence
**Tools:** Microsoft Excel, Power Query
**Dataset:** Superstore Sales Dataset (Kaggle)
**Project Focus:** Sales Performance, Profitability, Product Analysis, Customer Analysis, Regional Analysis and Discount Effectiveness

---

## Project Overview

Sales figures alone do not tell the full story of business performance. A business may generate high revenue while experiencing low profitability due to product costs, discounting decisions or poor performance across certain regions.

This project explores sales data to identify what drives revenue, which products and categories contribute most to profit, where performance is strongest, and where improvements may be needed.

Using Microsoft Excel and Power Query, I prepared and analyzed a Superstore dataset and developed an Executive Sales Performance Dashboard to communicate findings and support data-driven business decisions.

## Business Problem

The business needs a clearer understanding of its sales performance beyond total revenue.

Key questions explored include:

* Which products generate the highest sales and profit?
* Which products generate revenue but contribute little or negatively to profit?
* Which categories and subcategories perform best?
* Which regions, states and cities contribute most to sales and profitability?
* How do sales and profit change over time?
* How do different discount levels relate to profitability?
* Which customers appear most frequently in the transaction records?

## Project Objectives

1. Analyze overall sales, profit, quantity sold and discount performance.
2. Identify the best-performing products, categories and subcategories.
3. Evaluate performance across regions, states, cities and customer segments.
4. Examine monthly, quarterly and yearly sales trends.
5. Explore the relationship between discounts, sales and profitability.
6. Examine customer purchase frequency.
7. Develop an executive dashboard and provide business recommendations.

## Key Findings

### 1. Product Performance

The Canon imageCLASS 2200 Advanced Copier was the leading product highlighted in the analysis, generating approximately **$61,599.82 in sales** and **$25,199.93 in profit**.

The analysis also identified products generating sales without contributing positively to profitability.

### 2. Category Performance

Technology was the leading category, generating approximately:

* **Sales:** $836,154
* **Profit:** $145,454

Furniture generated substantial sales but comparatively low profit, highlighting the importance of evaluating profitability alongside revenue.

### 3. Subcategory Performance

* Phones recorded the highest sales at approximately **$330,007**.
* Copiers generated the highest profit at approximately **$55,618**.
* Tables, Bookcases and Supplies showed profitability concerns.

### 4. Regional Performance

The West region recorded the highest sales and profit in the analysis:

* **Sales:** Approximately $725,458
* **Profit:** Approximately $108,418

The Central region showed the weakest profitability among the regions examined.

### 5. State and City Performance

California led the state-level analysis, generating approximately **$457,688 in sales** and **$76,381 in profit**.

Texas recorded approximately $170,188 in sales but a loss of approximately $25,729.

New York City was the leading city in both sales and profit.

### 6. Customer Segment Performance

The Consumer segment contributed the highest sales and profit:

* **Sales:** Approximately $1.16 million
* **Profit:** Approximately $134,119

The Home Office segment contributed the least to both sales and profit.

### 7. Sales Trends

The analysis identified Q4 2017 as the strongest quarter in the period examined.

The sharp decline in the 2018 figures requires further investigation to determine whether it reflects incomplete data or a genuine reduction in sales activity.

### 8. Discount Analysis

The 0% discount group recorded the highest aggregate sales and profit in the analysis.

The 20% discount group also generated substantial sales and profit. Higher discount groups showed negative aggregate profit in the report.

These findings highlight the importance of monitoring discounting decisions and their relationship with profitability. They should be interpreted as aggregate comparisons rather than proof that discounting alone caused the profit differences.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following actions were recommended:

* Review inventory planning for consistently strong-performing products.
* Give greater attention to profitable categories such as Technology.
* Review pricing, supplier costs and performance for loss-making products.
* Introduce appropriate discount approval and monitoring processes.
* Focus marketing efforts on valuable customer segments.
* Strengthen customer retention initiatives.
* Plan inventory around periods of stronger demand.
* Use the executive dashboard for continuous performance monitoring.

## Project Limitations and Further Validation

The findings represent patterns in the dataset and should not automatically be interpreted as proof of causation.

The discount analysis is based on aggregate comparisons. Further analysis would be needed to isolate the effect of discounting from other factors.

The 2018 sales decline also requires additional investigation to establish whether the available data covers a complete year.
